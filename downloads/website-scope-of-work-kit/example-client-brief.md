# Example client brief — sanitised notes

> **FICTIONAL EXAMPLE.** Harbourline Physiotherapy does not exist. This brief
> was written to demonstrate the scoping workflow. No real client information
> appears anywhere in this kit.

These are the notes as they come out of a first call: incomplete, a bit
contradictory, with the important questions unasked. That is normal. The job of
the scope document is to turn this into something both sides can check.

---

## Call notes — 14 March, 40 minutes

**Who:** Harbourline Physiotherapy. Two clinics, one town. Six practitioners.
Speaking with the practice manager; the owner "will need to sign off".

**Why now:** the current site is eight years old, built by "someone's nephew",
and cannot be updated by the practice. They cannot change the class timetable
without emailing him, and he stopped replying in January.

**What they said they want**

- A site that "looks like a proper clinic, not a template"
- To be able to update the timetable themselves
- More enquiries. Currently "a few a week, mostly phone"
- "Maybe online booking eventually"
- A blog "at some point, for SEO"

**Content**

- Existing copy is "mostly fine but dated". They think they can rewrite it.
- No professional photos. Practitioner headshots are phone photos of varying
  quality.
- Logo exists as a JPEG. Nobody knows where the original is.

**Constraints mentioned**

- Wants it live "before the summer"
- Budget mentioned as "we were thinking a few thousand" — not pinned down
- Domain is registered somewhere, the manager is unsure where
- They have Google Analytics "I think, the nephew set it up"

**Not asked on the call, and it matters**

- Who is the single approver? The manager said the owner signs off but the
  owner was not on the call.
- Is the timetable one table or per-clinic?
- Do the two clinics need separate contact details, addresses, opening hours?
- Is there any patient data involved anywhere? (Stated: no. Worth confirming
  in writing.)
- What happens to the old site's URLs?
- Who writes the copy, really?

---

## What a scope must not do with these notes

Three things in the brief are **not** requirements yet, and writing them into a
scope as if they were is the origin of most disputes:

1. **"Maybe online booking eventually."** This is a wish, not a requirement. It
   belongs in exclusions, explicitly, so that "eventually" does not quietly
   become "in this project".
2. **"A blog at some point."** Same. Naming it as excluded is a kindness to
   both sides.
3. **"Before the summer."** A date with no fixed inputs behind it. Until the
   copy deadline is agreed, a delivery date is a guess with a signature on it.

The scope document turns the first two into named exclusions and the third into
a milestone that states what it depends on.
