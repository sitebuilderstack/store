# Website scope of work kit

Four documents that work as one connected example: a messy client brief, the
scope drafted from it, the change request that arrives mid-project, and the
blank template underneath.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `website-scope-of-work-template.md` | The blank template. Fourteen sections. |
| `example-client-brief.md` | Sanitised first-call notes — incomplete and a bit contradictory, as they always are. |
| `completed-website-scope-example.md` | The scope drafted from those notes. |
| `scope-change-request-example.md` | Three requests arriving in one email, classified and answered. |

Read them in that order. The change request only makes sense against the scope,
and the scope only makes sense against the brief.

**Everything here is fictional.** Harbourline Physiotherapy does not exist.
Names, dates and effort figures were invented to demonstrate the workflow, and
the costs are **not** market rates.

**This is operational project planning, not a legal agreement.** It records what
will be built and how both sides know it is done. It is not a contract and does
not replace one.

Free to use and adapt. From [SiteBuilderStack](https://sitebuilderstack.com).

## The one idea

A scope document is not there to protect you in a dispute. It is there so the
dispute does not happen — by writing down, before anyone is invested, the things
that are otherwise discovered at the worst moment: how many pages, who supplies
the copy, what counts as a revision, and what "done" looks like in a way both
sides can check.

The three sections that do most of that work are the ones people skip:

- **Exclusions.** Everything the client said "maybe" about, named. In the
  example, "online booking" is excluded in writing in March, which is why a
  request for it in May takes a paragraph to answer instead of a week.
- **Assumptions.** What you decided without being told. When one turns out to be
  wrong, you annotate it with the date rather than deleting it.
- **Open questions.** Facts you do not have. A visible TBD with an owner and a
  date is infinitely better than a plausible guess.

## Consistency

The example documents agree with each other on purpose: nine pages, three
service pages sharing a layout, two review rounds, the same seven functional
requirements, the same exclusions. If you adapt them, keep them agreeing — a
sample pack whose documents contradict each other teaches the opposite of what
it is for.
