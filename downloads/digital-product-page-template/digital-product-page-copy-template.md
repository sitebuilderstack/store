# Digital product page — copy and layout template

A writing template for a Shopify product detail page selling a **downloadable**
item: a toolkit, a template pack, a set of workflows, a guide, a font, a preset.

**What this is:** a copy-and-layout template. Fill in the prose, then build it
with whatever your theme already gives you.

**What this is not:** a Shopify theme, an app, or a Liquid section. There is no
file here to install. Nothing is generated.

Free to use and adapt. From [SiteBuilderStack](https://sitebuilderstack.com).

---

## How to use it

Each section below has a **purpose**, a **shape**, and a **fill-in**. Replace
everything in `[square brackets]`. Delete any section your product genuinely
does not need — an empty section is worse than no section.

Two rules that make the difference between this working and not:

1. **Every claim must be checkable by someone who has not bought it.** If you
   cannot point at where a number comes from, cut it.
2. **Write the requirements before the benefits.** A buyer who discovers the
   prerequisite after paying is a refund request or a chargeback, whichever
   your policy allows.

---

## 1. Product identity and headline

**Purpose:** in one line, what the buyer will be able to do.

**Shape:** outcome first, category second. Not the file count. Not "ultimate".

```
[Product name]

[Verb the buyer performs] [the thing they get] [without the failure they fear].
```

**Fill-in example**

> **Conversion & Revenue Optimization Toolkit**
> Find where visitors get stuck, decide what to fix first, and check whether
> the fix worked.

**Avoid:** "The ultimate all-in-one toolkit", "10,000+ words", "Everything you
need". None of these tells a buyer what changes on Monday.

---

## 2. Short description

**Purpose:** the paragraph a skimmer reads before deciding to keep reading.

**Shape:** who it is for, the problem, the mechanism. Three sentences.

```
For [specific role] who [specific situation].
[The problem, stated as they would state it.]
[What the product does about it — the mechanism, not the adjective.]
```

**Fill-in example**

> For developers and founders who own a site that gets traffic and does not
> turn enough of it into revenue. The honest answer to "why" usually needs
> evidence nobody has collected. This is thirteen modules that separate the
> audit from the plan, the plan from the change, and the change from the claim
> that it worked.

---

## 3. Three concrete benefits

**Purpose:** three reasons, each specific enough to be wrong.

**Shape:** one bolded claim, one sentence of substance. Not nine bullets.

```
- **[Claim with a noun in it]** — [the detail that makes it checkable].
- **[Claim]** — [detail].
- **[Claim]** — [detail].
```

**Test:** could a competitor put the identical bullet on their page? If yes, it
says nothing. "Saves you time" fails. "Refuses to run an A/B test the traffic
cannot support, and tells you the threshold" passes.

---

## 4. Price and purchase action

**Purpose:** no hunting.

**Rules:**

- Price rendered from the live product, never typed into the description. A
  hard-coded price is wrong the first time you change it.
- The button label matches its behaviour. *Add to cart* adds. *Buy now* enters
  checkout. Neither of them is "Complete purchase".
- One primary action. Everything else on the page is visually quieter.
- State the currency, and that it is a one-time payment if it is one.

---

## 5. Genuine preview

**Purpose:** show the output, not the packaging.

**Shape:** one real artefact the product produces — a report, a filled-in
template, a worked example, an excerpt — rendered as readable text or shown in
a real screenshot.

**Why this section decides the sale:** a screenshot of a folder tree tells a
buyer how much there is. An actual finished report tells them whether it is any
good. Only one of those answers the question they are actually asking.

```
### What it produces

[One-line description of the artefact.]

[The artefact itself: an excerpt, a table, a rendered example — long enough to
judge, short enough to read. Label it: real output / worked example /
fictional demonstration.]
```

**Do not:** invent a dashboard, mock up a UI the product does not have, or
show a chart of results nobody measured.

---

## 6. Deliverables and formats

**Purpose:** what lands in the buyer's downloads folder.

```
### What you receive

- [File type and count, e.g. "One ZIP, 99 files"]
- [Format, e.g. "Plain Markdown, readable in any editor"]
- [Anything executable, named: "four Python scripts, standard library only"]
- [Total size]
- [Checksum, if you publish one]
```

Counts belong **here**, with context — not in the headline. "99 files" answers
"how much" only after the buyer knows what one of them does.

---

## 7. First-use walkthrough

**Purpose:** remove the fear of buying something that sits unopened.

```
### Your first twenty minutes

1. [What to open first, by filename.]
2. [What information or access to supply.]
3. [Which workflow to run.]
4. [What output to expect, described concretely.]
5. [What you must review before acting on it.]
```

If you cannot write step 4 concretely, the product is not ready to sell.

---

## 8. Requirements

**Purpose:** the buyer self-qualifies before paying, not after.

**Put this above the fold or immediately after the first purchase action. Never
in a collapsed accordion.**

```
### What you need already

- [Software they must have and you do not supply.]
- [Runtime, with a version, if anything executes.]
- [Access or permissions they must be able to grant.]
- [Skill level, stated plainly.]
- [Anything explicitly NOT included.]
```

"No dependencies" is almost always false. The archive may install nothing while
still requiring a terminal, a runtime, and a paid account somewhere else.

---

## 9. Licence and delivery

```
### Licence and delivery

- Delivery: [when and how the file arrives].
- Licence: [who may use it, for what, and whether client work is included].
- Not permitted: [redistribution, resale — state it plainly].
- Refunds: [link to your actual policy. Do not summarise it into something
  more generous or more restrictive than it says.]
```

The refund line is the one that goes stale. Link to the policy and let the
policy be the source of truth.

---

## 10. Support and limitations

**Purpose:** credibility. A page that names a limitation is more believable
than one that does not.

```
### What this does not do

- [The adjacent problem it does not solve, and what does.]
- [The outcome it cannot promise.]
- [Who should not buy it.]

### Support

[What is actually included. If that is "email me and I will answer", say that.
Do not imply a helpdesk you do not run.]
```

---

## 11. FAQs

Three to six. Each must answer a question a real buyer asks before paying —
not a question invented to hold a keyword.

Good ones to steal: *Do I need [prerequisite]?* · *How is it delivered?* ·
*Can I use it for client work?* · *What if it is not what I expected?* ·
*How is this different from [the free thing]?*

---

## 12. Final purchase action

Repeat: what it is, the live price, the same button label, the same product.

**The failure to avoid:** a closing call to action that promotes a *different*
product. If a cross-sell belongs on the page, it goes after this, styled
quieter, and labelled as a different product.

---

## The two-minute check

- [ ] Headline states an outcome, not a file count.
- [ ] Requirements appear before the second purchase action, uncollapsed.
- [ ] Price comes from the product, not typed into the copy.
- [ ] One real artefact is visible without paying.
- [ ] Every number can be traced to something.
- [ ] Button labels match behaviour.
- [ ] The closing action sells *this* product.
- [ ] Nothing implies a hosted service if it is a download.
