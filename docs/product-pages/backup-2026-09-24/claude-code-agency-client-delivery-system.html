Turn Claude Code Into Your Client Delivery System.

A complete operating system for freelancers and agencies to discover, scope, propose, build, review, launch, hand off and retain website clients — as Claude Code commands that read one set of files per client and write into them, with a person between every draft and the client. Not a folder of proposal prompts: 21 modules, 37 commands, 29 templates plus a client-project skeleton, 17 checklists, 12 report shapes, 3 worksheets, four small scripts, and one complete fictional engagement to read before you run your own.

The problem

Most developers know how to build a website. The engagement around it is where the money and the reputation go: a proposal improvised the night before, a scope that said “and anything else needed”, an estimate that was a guess, revisions that never end because nobody counted the rounds, documentation that lives in a chat thread, a handoff that is a folder of files, and no process for turning a finished project into next month’s income. Each engagement starts from scratch; each one teaches the same lesson again.

Ask a capable model to “write a proposal” and you get the other problem: confident prose with invented requirements, a budget the client never stated, a promise about rankings, and a signature block that looks like a contract. Speed without a system produces professional-looking documents that are not professional.

The solution

A structured delivery system that runs inside Claude Code:

DISCOVER → SCOPE → PROPOSE → ONBOARD → BUILD → REVIEW → LAUNCH → HANDOFF → RETAIN
  • One set of files per client — client-config.yaml for the facts, CLAUDE.md for the rules, the scope as the contract, and four logs (decisions, assumptions, changes, risks) as the memory. Every command reads them; none invents.
  • Claude Code commands for every stage, each marked INTERNAL USE or CLIENT-FACING, each with its purpose, inputs, required context, steps, business rules, output format and validation — from /lead-qualification to /retainer-opportunity, with /agency-project-status as the one-block summary of any engagement.
  • Scope discipline as a habit: counts in the scope, an out-of-scope list before the price, acceptance criteria you can test, and /scope-change-check that classifies any new request in a minute with the reply drafted — calm, because it is arithmetic.
  • Evidence over confidence: audits with evidence and a five-level priority system, estimates as ranges with assumptions, readiness with proof, reports where every number names its source and missing data says unavailable.
  • The ten operating rules in every command: work within scope, never invent client facts, identify uncertainty, preserve what works, expose no credentials, document changes, validate before done, flag scope creep, promise nothing the evidence does not support, stay professional.

Safety and honesty

No credentials in any file, ever: access is granted by platform invitation and recorded as present or missing. No invented client facts, budgets or metrics: TBD is a value, and a report says unavailable rather than guessing. No promised rankings, conversion increases, revenue, or launch dates while an input is outstanding. Nothing is sent to a client by a command; a person reads and sends. Testimonials are asked for honestly and never written for the client; case studies use only the facts in the files.

And plainly: this is not legal, accounting or tax advice. Proposals are not contracts, scope documents do not replace agreements, retainer templates describe services rather than terms, and the pricing examples are hypothetical reasoning, not rates.

  2. The first ten minutes

    Copy the commands, scripts and templates into your agency folder; create the client project with the script (it never overwrites); fill the config from what you know; run discovery.

    cp .claude/commands/*.md your-agency/.claude/commands/
cp -r scripts templates your-agency/
cd your-agency
python3 scripts/new_client.py "Northwind Supply" --project "Website Redesign" --platform wordpress --type redesign
  created clients/northwind-supply (28 files). Next: fill client-config.yaml, then /lead-qualification or /client-discovery

cd clients/northwind-supply && claude
> /client-discovery

  3. Qualify before you spend unpaid hours

    The intake is quoted, not paraphrased; a budget the prospect did not state stays blank. The score comes with reasons, the unknowns become the next call's questions, and WEAK means "not on these terms", not "no".

    LEAD QUALIFICATION

Client:   Harbourline Physio (director)
Project:  Website redesign, WordPress, same domain

FIT SCORE: STRONG

Project Type:          Redesign; SEO preservation; booking conversion
Estimated Complexity:  M

Unknowns:
- Which pages carry the organic traffic (Search Console)
- Who holds hosting and registrar access

Recommended Next Step:
Book discovery; request Search Console/GA4 access first.

  4. Audit with evidence, then scope from it

    Every finding has an issue, evidence, business impact, recommendation, effort and a priority from CRITICAL to OPPORTUNITY. The priority actions become the candidate scope; the executive summary is two pages the client reads.

    ## Priority actions
| # | Action                                        | Priority | Effort |
| 1 | Booking CTA on every page; widget above fold  | HIGH     | build  |
| 2 | Preserve the two URLs with 61% of clicks      | HIGH     | S      |
| 3 | Rebuild templates for mobile (LCP 5.8 s)      | HIGH     | M      |
| 4 | Unique titles; merge 4 thin pages with 301s   | HIGH     | S      |

Not checked: server logs; the booking widget's own analytics.

  5. A scope with counts and an out-of-scope list

    In scope is counted (pages, templates, rounds, forms). Out of scope names what the client will think of later. Client responsibilities carry dates and the day-for-day rule. Every deliverable has a criterion someone can test.

    IN SCOPE
3. Design for the five page templates, desktop and mobile, 2 revision rounds
4. Build as a child theme: 11 pages plus the profile template

OUT OF SCOPE
1. Copywriting beyond light drafts of titles/descriptions
4. Online payments, deposits, or a client portal
9. Maintenance beyond the 30-day defect window

CLIENT RESPONSIBILITIES
- Final copy for all 11 pages by 30 Oct; each business day
  late moves launch by one business day

  6. An estimate that is a range

    Work items with low, expected and high hours and a note per row saying what each assumes. The script totals by phase, applies contingency as a line, and prints the assumptions. The proposal quotes the range or the high — never the low alone.

    $ python3 scripts/estimate.py project-estimation.csv --contingency 15 --rate 90
PROJECT ESTIMATE — 18 work item(s)
phase              low  expected   high
Design            29.5      33.0   49.5
Development       30.0      34.5   50.0
...
TOTAL             95.5     135.8  205.2   hours before contingency
                  95.5     156.1  236.0   with 15% contingency
at 90.00/hour:    8595     14050  21243

ASSUMPTIONS (from the worksheet notes):
  - Page assembly: 11 pages; assumes copy on time

  7. Scope creep, classified in a minute

    Every new request is checked against the scope text — quoted, not remembered — and classified IN SCOPE, AMBIGUOUS or OUT OF SCOPE with the four impacts and the reply drafted. Not every change costs money; every change is logged.

    REQUEST: "Could clients pay for the assessment when they book?"
ORIGINAL SCOPE: out-of-scope item 4 "Online payments, deposits,
  or a patient/client portal"

CLASSIFICATION: OUT OF SCOPE

ESTIMATED IMPACT: +10 to +15 business days before launch;
  vendor add-on (client's) + 8–14h configuration; none if phase 2
RECOMMENDED RESPONSE: /change-request CR-02; recommend phase 2
  so the 9 December date holds.

  8. The Friday report, from the tracker

    Overall status with the one-sentence reason; completed, in progress, next week; the client's actions in their own section with dates and consequences; risks; decisions needed; the launch date and why it moved. Ten minutes from the files.

    PROJECT STATUS — week of 2 November
Overall Status: AT RISK — copy for three service pages is
  outstanding since 30 October

Client Actions Needed:
  Approve design round 2            Fri 6 Nov   build starts Monday
  Copy for the three service pages  Fri 7 Nov   launch 9 Dec holds

Launch target: 4 December → 9 December if the copy
arrives by 7 November (day-for-day rule).

  9. Launch on evidence

    Thirteen checks with the file, the log entry or the test that proves each: scope, requirements, QA on the production build, written approval, backup, DNS, SSL, redirects, SEO, analytics, forms, environment, rollback. Any blocker is NO-GO regardless of the date.

    LAUNCH RECOMMENDATION: GO WITH WARNINGS
Decided by RL (recommendation) and the director (approval 8 Dec 16:20)

| Client approval   | PASS | decision-log 2026-12-03; 8 Dec email |
| Backup            | PASS | host 8 Dec 09:00; restore tested 2 Dec |
| Redirects         | WARN | 4 rules staged; verified at T+15m |
| Rollback plan     | PASS | previous theme kept; 15 minutes; RL |

Blockers: none

  10. The whole engagement in one block

    From the config, the tracker, the action items, the risks and the change log: phase, status, every stage, blockers, client actions, risks, the next five actions — and project-status.json for whatever you build on top.
