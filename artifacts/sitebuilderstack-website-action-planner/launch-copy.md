### Benefit-focused body

> **Most website advice fails at the same place: it does not know your
> situation.** "Improve your SEO" is not a task. "Optimise conversions" is not
> a task. Neither survives contact with a site that is not live yet, or one
> nobody has found, or a catalogue of 500 products that needs the same edit.
>
> The Website Action Planner asks what you are actually trying to do, what the
> site runs on, and where it is today — then gives you three specific actions
> in the order they should be done. Each one says what you will have when it is
> finished and how to check that it is. No numbers are invented and nothing
> about your site is scanned; the plan is built from your answers and says so.
>
> **You also get a starter Claude Code prompt written for your situation.** It
> is bounded: it reads and reports, it changes nothing, it demands evidence for
> every finding, and it stops before anything destructive. It is useful whether
> or not you ever buy anything, and it is yours to edit and keep.
>
> **And it will tell you not to buy.** If your site is not live, it will not
> sell you conversion work. If nobody is arriving yet, it will not sell you
> conversion work either. If you are not yet comfortable in a terminal, it
> gives you a free path to get there first. If you already own the product that
> fits, it says so and shows you how to use it. The recommendation, when there
> is one, names the answers it came from and states a reason it might not be
> for you.

### The three things it produces

1. **Three prioritised actions** — what to do, why it matters for your
   situation, what you will have at the end, and how to verify it is done.
2. **A free starter Claude Code prompt** — scoped to your goal and platform,
   read-only, evidence-demanding, with an explicit stopping point.
3. **One matched toolkit, or none** — with the price, what it includes, what it
   does not, what the free plan already covers, and a reason not to buy yet.

### FAQ

**Is it free?**
Yes, and the whole result is on one page. No email address is required to see
it, and nothing is held back behind a signup.

**Does it look at my website?**
No. It does not scan, crawl or audit anything. Every recommendation comes from
the answers you select, and the plan says so on every copy and export.

**Do I need Claude Code to use it?**
No — the plan is useful on its own. The starter prompt is written for Claude
Code, and if you are not set up yet the planner gives you a free path to get
there instead of recommending a purchase.

**Will it just try to sell me something?**
It recommends at most one product, and often none. It is made by
SiteBuilderStack and recommends SiteBuilderStack products — that is stated on
the first screen. It is not an independent review site.

**How long does it take?**
Six questions. We are not going to put a number of minutes on it until we have
watched people use it.

**Are the prices current?**
They are a snapshot with the date it was taken, shown on the result page. When
the snapshot passes its freshness window the planner stops showing prices and
links you to the product page instead. The storefront is always the authority.

> The thing I kept running into: most "what should I do next" advice ignores

> isn't indexed. People ask about SEO on a site that hasn't launched. People

> qualification before any recommendation:

> - On Shopify but bulk admin isn't your problem → no automation product.
> - Not comfortable in a terminal yet → a free getting-started path, no purchase.
> - Already own the matching product → how to use it, nothing to buy.
> - Two products needed? It does the arithmetic and tells you the bundle is one
>   cent *more*, not less.

> Code prompt for your situation — read-only, evidence-demanding, with an
