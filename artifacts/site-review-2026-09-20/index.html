sitebuilderstack.com · review of 20 September 2026

Is Site Builder Stack progressing, or should the project be deleted?

Every number below was read from an API today — Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, Shopify — or is stated as unavailable. Nothing is estimated. The site became visible to search engines on 27 August 2026, so this is a review of 24 days.

C+overall

Keep it. It is progressing — early, small, and in the right direction — and deleting it now would throw away the part that is working before the part that takes time has had time.

Is it growing and progressing in the right direction? Yes, on every leading indicator a 24-day-old site can show: search clicks went from 0 to about one a day, average position moved from 55 to 35 in the last two weeks, new pages are indexed within two days of publishing, Bing's index of the site went from 4 pages to 33. What it does not yet show is the lagging indicators that would justify the effort commercially: a human audience (roughly 67 real visits in 30 days) and revenue (one order, on 28 August). The grade is a C+ because the build is excellent and the traction is not yet there; it is not a D because three weeks is too short for traction to be there.

The honest condition: this is a keep-for-90-days verdict with milestones, not a keep-forever verdict. The decision points are at the bottom.

Grades by area

A-

Technical execution

Every automated check is green: 55/55 offline, 152 rendered checks on live, a clean site-wide SEO crawl, accessibility clean at phone and desktop, LCP ≈ 0.5–0.9 s, CLS 0. Only pre-existing nit found in a month was a card heading size. This is the strongest thing about the project.

B+

36 long-form guides (most 2,000–6,000 words, tested examples, sourced), 4 labs, a weekly challenge series, 4 tools, 8 checklists, 8 products with real, audited contents. Weakness: much of it competes with Anthropic's own documentation for queries like "claude code hooks", where a new domain will not win.

The sitemap is read (85 URLs, 0 errors) so these are crawl-priority problems, not configuration ones.

1,694 impressions and 11 clicks in 28 days; average position 47.8; CTR 0.65%. Impressions sit at 40–70 a day after the Aug 29–30 burst. 90% of queries are past position 40. The two biggest query clusters ("claude code seo", "claude code hooks") are held at positions 51 and 80.

D

Audience

Real human visits are roughly the search sessions: ~67 in 30 days (Google 48, Bing 17, ChatGPT 2). The 1,858 "sessions" Shopify reports are ~96% direct and spike on the days the test suite ran (506, 310, 371) — that is automation, not people. Newsletter signups: 0.

D

Revenue

One real order, $99.00, on 28 August 2026 (The Launch System at its launch price). Nothing since, across eight products and $19.99–$39.99 price points. No evidence yet of paid demand; no evidence against it either — the pages have had almost no human visitors.

B

Trajectory

Every leading indicator has moved the right way in three weeks: clicks 3 → 8 per week, average position 55 → 35 since 7 September, first Bing clicks, indexed pages up, 12 new URLs indexed within two days of publishing. It is early and small, and it is pointed the right way.

Weighting: technical, content and trajectory are what you control and they are strong; visibility, audience and revenue are what the market controls and they are early. A C+ is "good build, unproven business, pointed the right way".

Why not delete it

Three reasons, in order of weight.

1. Time, not effort, is the missing ingredient. Google has known the domain for 24 days. New domains routinely spend two to four months between "indexed" and "ranking where the content deserves", and the positions here are moving in that direction on schedule: 55 → 35 in two weeks without any link building.

2. The asset is unusually complete for its age. 36 tested guides, 8 audited products, labs, tools, a challenge series, a clean technical footprint and a test suite that catches regressions. Deleting it destroys ~1,200 files of reviewed work to save a hosting bill; parking it costs almost nothing.

3. The one sale happened. Someone paid $99 for the flagship product on day two, before the price was lowered, before any guides ranked. That is one data point, but it is the right one: the product is buyable.

What would justify deleting it

If, after another 90 days of the site simply existing (no further build work), none of the milestones below are met, the honest conclusion is that this topic does not send buyers to a new domain, and the project should be wound down or repurposed. Decide on 19 December 2026 against these:

  • Visibility: ≥ 1,000 Google impressions per week and average position under 30 for the applied queries ("claude code seo", "connect shopify to claude code", "website launch checklist"). Today: ~400 per week, position 38.
  • Audience: ≥ 50 Google clicks per week (today 8) and ≥ 300 human visits per month across engines and AI referrers.
  • Indexation: every product page and hub indexed; the technical-SEO guide indexed. Today: 2 unknown, 1 discovered-not-indexed.
  • Revenue: at least 3 organic orders in the 90 days (any product). Today: 1, on day two.
  • Signal from the funnel: the SEO Audit Navigator and Launch Readiness Analyzer artifacts, once shared, send measurable arrivals (the store already records them with attribution). Today: 1 arrival, from testing.

Two of those five would be a clear keep; none would be a clear stop; in between, the decision is about whether you want to keep publishing.
