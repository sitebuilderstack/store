Paste a page's text. This checks the things that decide whether a model can find a self-contained answer in it, shows you the block it would most likely lift, and writes the llms.txt entry for it.

What this is based on. No answer engine publishes its extraction rules. These eighteen checks come from what is observable and uncontroversial: crawlability, whether the answer is in the HTML, whether a claim survives being lifted out of its paragraph, and whether the page names its own facts. Treat them as informed practice, not mechanics. Nothing here predicts a citation, and anyone selling you a guaranteed one is selling something that does not exist.

    Two things worth knowing
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Does this page itself get cited?
    
        
    No — and it is worth understanding why, because it applies to anything you publish on a platform rather than your own domain. claude.ai/robots.txt returns Disallow: / for GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User and Google-Extended, and the artifact origin sits behind a bot challenge. Checked 25 September 2026.
    
        
    A tool like this earns traffic by being shared, not by being crawled. The content you want cited has to live on a domain you control, in HTML, that AI crawlers are allowed to fetch.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Why the opening matters more than the length
    
        
    A model extracting an answer reads for a passage that stands alone. A sentence starting "As mentioned above, it does this by…" cannot be lifted, because the thing it refers to is not in the extract.
    
        
    That is why the checks below care about self-containment, resolved pronouns and named entities far more than about word count. Long does not help. Quotable does.
