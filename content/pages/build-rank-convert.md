## The order is not arbitrary

You cannot convert traffic you do not have, and you cannot rank a site that does
not exist. That sounds obvious and it is routinely ignored: the most common
wasted quarter in web work is optimising conversion on a page nobody reaches, or
optimising titles on URLs that are about to be canonicalised away.

So the order matters, and knowing which stage you are actually in matters more
than any individual tactic.

## Which one are you in?

The honest test is what happens when you look at your own numbers.

**If you cannot answer "is it live and does it pass its own checks"** — you are
at Build. Everything downstream is premature.

**If it is live and Search Console shows few impressions** — you are at Rank.
Conversion work on 40 sessions a month cannot be measured, and any change you
make will be indistinguishable from noise.

**If impressions and sessions are real but nothing happens** — you are at
Convert, and the first job is finding out where people stop rather than guessing
at fixes.

**If it is live, found and converting, and the question is keeping it that way**
— you are at Operate. This is the stage that lasts longest, and the one where
the failures are quiet: a certificate that stops renewing, a form that stops
delivering, a plugin with a published exploit, a backup nobody has restored.
The work is a rhythm of checks, not a project.

**If the site has to move** — a new platform, a new host, a new domain, a new
URL structure — you are at Migrate, and the risk is not the build but what
gets lost on the way: URLs, redirects, titles, images, forms, analytics. The
work is an inventory, a map and a before/after comparison, with a go/no-go at
the end and a rollback that was prepared before the DNS change. It is a
project with a start and an end, unlike Operate; it is also the point where
most sites quietly lose a year of search traffic.

**If you build websites for clients** — and the site is fine but the
engagement is not: the scope that drifted, the proposal written the night
before, the revisions that never end, the handoff that was a folder — you are
at Deliver. That is not a stage of the website; it is the operating system of
the business that builds them, and it is solved once and reused on every
client.

**If the store runs on Shopify and the catalogue is the work** — products,
collections, SEO, metafields, inventory and redirects at a scale the admin
cannot keep up with — that is not a stage but a platform. The Shopify
Automation & Admin API Toolkit drives the Admin API from Claude Code, with a
preview before every write.

**If you own the first three for more than one site** — you are running the
lifecycle, not a stage, and the useful thing is a repeatable system rather than
three separate purchases.
